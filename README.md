On RackNerd, the promotional price being the renewal price is the company's most-repeated selling point, and it's backed by the company's own forum replies plus users reporting four or five consecutive renewals at the same rate. ## Speed and stability: what the published tests actually show

Independent reviews of two JustHost nodes give a consistent picture on disk. The Fujairah (UAE, DXB1) node, on an Intel Xeon Gold 6148 with BBR enabled, measured roughly 603 MB/s sequential I/O, about 64 MB/s on 4K read/write, and around 16,100 IOPS. The São Paulo (Brazil, SAO1) node, on an E5-2699v4, came in at about 509 MB/s, 82 MB/s on 4K, and 20,600 IOPS. Those are ordinary-good NVMe numbers, in a tight band.

Latency is where the location choice bites. The Brazil node shows high latency to all three Chinese networks, and the reviewer recommends transit if that's your traffic. The UAE node does well on China Mobile, acceptably on China Unicom, and struggles on China Telecom at peak hours. If you're buying a Dubai box to serve mainland users, plan for a relay.

RackNerd's disk numbers are all over the place, and the spread tells you more than any single figure. One community benchmark on a Dublin Black Friday box recorded over 40 MB/s sequential write with 11,000+ IOPS in sync/direct mode; a separate 2026 write-up reports roughly 1.93 GB/s mixed throughput and about 36,000 combined 4K IOPS. Same brand, wildly different results, because you're on a shared node and the node you land on matters more than the logo. Uptime monitoring across multiple accounts has shown 99.9%–99.993%.

Rather than trust any of these, test the box you actually get. It takes two minutes:


dd if=/dev/zero of=/tmp/bench bs=1M count=2048 conv=fdatasync status=progress
sysbench cpu --cpu-max-prime=20000 run
fio --name=randrw --filename=/tmp/fio-test --size=1G --rw=randrw --bs=4k --iodepth=32 --direct=1 --time_based --runtime=30 --group_reporting


Watch CPU steal during the sysbench run as closely as the score. A cheap node with steal above a few percent at idle is a node someone else is maxing out.

## Who should pick which

**Take RackNerd if:**
- You're in North America or Europe and want a cheap always-on box for WireGuard, monitoring, a Git mirror, a small blog, or a staging environment.
- You can live with a 2 TB monthly transfer cap and a shared 1 Gbps port.
- You're paying annually and want the lowest possible number on the invoice.

**Take JustHost if:**
- You need a UAE or Brazil IP. Native IPs on those nodes unlock local TikTok, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Netflix, per the independent tests above.
- Your workload is bandwidth-heavy but not latency-sensitive: 300–750 Mbps unmetered with a 93–232 TB monthly equivalent beats a 2–9 TB cap.
- You want to move a VM between datacenters or swap its IP without rebuilding. The panel does both, and one review notes up to 50 IP switches per VM.
- You want to pay monthly, or you want to pay with Alipay or UnionPay.
- You only need IPv6 and want to spend as little as possible.

**Neither fits if** you need managed services, an SLA with credits, Kubernetes, load balancing, object storage, or anything where an hour of downtime costs real money. Both are self-managed boxes sold on price.

## Before you order, confirm five things

1. **Is the promo still in stock?** RackNerd's promotional tiers sell out and revert to regular pricing; JustHost's "Special" variants rotate the same way. Check the price at checkout, not in a review.
2. **Which datacenter are you getting?** On JustHost, the location is part of the product. Pricing and latency differ by node, and the UAE and Brazil boxes are not interchangeable.
3. **What's the minimum term?** JustHost shows lower rates for 12-month payment, and some plans require three months minimum. A "monthly" price you can't actually buy for one month isn't monthly.
4. **What does renewal cost?** Get the number for your specific term in writing, from either host, before you commit.
5. **Who's doing backups?** Neither includes managed backup as standard. Budget for off-box snapshots or a second cheap VPS, because a $33/year server with no backup is still a $33/year way to lose your data.

If your project needs a specific country's IP or unmetered bandwidth at a mid-tier port speed, JustHost is the only one of the two selling it, and the 1 GB / 20 GB NVMe plan at $2.25/month on a 12-month term is the sensible entry point. If you just want a Linux box that stays up for less than the price of a coffee per year, buy the RackNerd annual deal and spend the difference on backups.
